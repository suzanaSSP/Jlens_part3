Claude's advice on the distribution:
Given the pooling method we settled on (average positions within a question first, then average across questions), each of your 70 units isn't a single noisy token draw — it's already a smoothed summary over ~hundreds of thought tokens. So the effective noise per unit is lower than raw N=70 would suggest for, say, 70 independent single-token samples. That's the good news.
Why it might not be enough anyway — depends on what you're separating. Distinct categories (sports vs. religion) probably diverge enough that even 70 (or the ~49 you'd use after a split) shows a clean signal — the effect size is large relative to sampling noise. The comparison you actually care about (does "ethics" sit unusually close to "religious") is a subtler effect, and subtle effects need more data to distinguish reliably from noise. 70 might show you a difference, but the confidence interval around it could be wide enough that you can't be sure it's real rather than an artifact of which particular 49 questions ended up in the ground truth.
Concrete way to answer this yourself instead of trusting a rule of thumb: run a convergence/stability check. Build the ground truth incrementally — from 10 questions, 20, 30, ... up to whatever you hold out for training — and measure how much the distribution itself changes each time you add a batch (e.g. divergence between ground-truth-at-N and ground-truth-at-N-10, or bootstrap resampling variance at your final N). If the distribution is still moving substantially as you add the last 10-20 questions, that's your direct evidence you haven't saturated yet for that category. If it's basically flat by ~50, 70 is plenty for that category specifically. This also naturally answers the question per-category rather than assuming one number applies to all of them — your 19-question categories will very likely still be climbing on this curve, while a 70-question category might already be flat.
Bottom line: I'd treat 70 as fine to build a first-pass pipeline and validate it end-to-end, but I wouldn't treat a subtle cross-category finding (like the ethics/religion overlap) as solid until either the stability check shows convergence or you've bumped up the question count for that specific comparison.
