Step 3 — average across questions to get the category ground truth
Question A had 3 positions, Question B had 2 — but that no longer matters, because each question already collapsed to one vector. Now it's just a plain average of two vectors:

God	sin	law	court	code	residual
Q A	.233	.100	.233	.300	.033	.100
Q B	.100	0	.400	.200	.200	.100
GT	.167	.050	.317	.250	.117	.100
That last row is the category's ground truth distribution. Add a 3rd question and it's (A + B + C) / 3, elementwise, same way.

Why the two-stage average matters: if you'd instead thrown all 5 positions (3 from A + 2 from B) into one big average, Question A would get 60% of the weight just for being longer, even though it's only 1 of 2 questions. Averaging within-question first, then across questions, makes each question count equally regardless of how many tokens it generated.

Scale this up: real vocab instead of 5 tokens, top-20 instead of top-3, and dozens of questions instead of 2 — same two-step mechanics, just bigger vectors.


Category - baseline mean #nowitmakessense
most of the ground truth distributions overlap. So when computing the differences between the distributions, I saw most of the categories behaving the same way, increasing and decreasing the same way. 
That's because most distributions overlap in same token words. To avoid that a baseline mean was created, and that is subtracted from the main category distribution. 
The baseline mean was created by finding the mean similarity baseline of Law/Politics/Government/History, Technology & Definitions, Science/Physics/Math
Chosen because they're presumed to have no plausible category-specific content in these moral-reasoning answers. Subtracting that mean removes the shared/generic component, leaving what's specific to the category in question.
